# Draft Abstract

Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) offer a way to connect neural-network training with governing equations from scientific simulation, but their practical behaviour depends strongly on how the competing parts of the loss function are balanced. This project studies adaptive PINN training for CFD-oriented Czochralski silicon single-crystal growth simulation. A baseline PINN, a gradient-normalized PINN, and an agentic adaptive PINN controller are compared on heat-equation and simplified crystal-growth-oriented PDE experiments. The results show that adaptive control can reduce PDE residuals in selected cases, but this improvement can introduce trade-offs with boundary or initial-condition accuracy. The project therefore evaluates models through component-level physics losses rather than total loss alone. To connect the simplified PINN study with simulation data, corrected Czochralski CFD sweep datasets are also used for fast Gaussian Process Regression surrogate evaluation. The GPR surrogate provides case-wise holdout predictions together with uncertainty maps, helping identify regions where the surrogate is less confident. Overall, the work supports a careful use of adaptive physics-informed training for crystal-growth-oriented scientific machine learning, while emphasizing interpretability, uncertainty, and reproducible evaluation instead of claiming full replacement of high-fidelity CFD solvers.
